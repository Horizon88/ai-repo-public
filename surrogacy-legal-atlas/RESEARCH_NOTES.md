# Global Surrogacy Legal Atlas - Research Notes

Updated: 2026-08-17

This is an initial research map, not legal advice. Surrogacy law changes quickly and frequently depends on:

- citizenship and residence of the intended parents
- marital status and sex/gender of the intended parents
- whether at least one intended parent is genetically related to the child
- whether the arrangement is gestational or traditional surrogacy
- whether payment is compensation, reimbursement, or prohibited consideration
- pre-birth versus post-birth parentage orders
- immigration, passport, and exit-document practice
- recognition of parentage in the home country

## Source Selection

Preferred sources are official government or legislative materials. Where English primary sources were not readily available, the v1 dataset uses a conservative note and flags the jurisdiction as medium or high risk.

Key sources used in the site:

- Health Canada: surrogacy payment and reimbursement prohibitions.
- GOV.UK and Cafcass: UK parental orders and unenforceability of surrogacy agreements.
- Australian Smartraveller: state/territory criminal risk and international-surrogacy warnings.
- European Parliament Think Tank 2025 briefing: EU country legal posture.
- California Family Code, Nevada NRS, and New York Department of Health: US state examples.
- Smartraveller country pages: Argentina, Georgia, Mexico, Ukraine warnings.
- India Surrogacy Regulation Act 2021 and academic review.
- Thailand ART Act review.
- South Africa Children's Act legal review.

## V1 Editorial Stance

- "Commercial / compensated" means a paid route appears legally available if structured correctly. It does not mean low-risk.
- "Altruistic only" means reimbursement may be allowed but compensation for carrying the child is restricted or criminal.
- "Grey" means the country is used in practice but lacks a stable, protective national framework.
- "Closed" means not a viable foreign-intended-parent route or creates criminal/prohibition exposure.
- "Risk" is practical legal risk, not moral judgment.
